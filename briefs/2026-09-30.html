This morning, the August PCE inflation report came in cooler than feared, offering a brief window of relief — but with end-of-quarter rebalancing, a $2 trillion Anthropic IPO in the headlines, and Friday's jobs report looming, the relief may be short-lived.

The whole issue in a paragraph

The 10-year Treasury yield touched 5.29% intraday Tuesday — its highest since 2007 — forcing the S&P 500 to a second consecutive decline (−0.17% to 7,670.84) and pushing the Dow down 131 points to 51,349.92 while the Nasdaq barely held above water at −0.09%. The twin drivers are the ongoing U.S.-Iran conflict, which is keeping oil above $90/barrel and stoking inflation fears, and the Federal Reserve's higher-for-longer posture, which is repricing every asset on earth. This morning brought a critical data release: August PCE inflation printed +3.4% YoY and core PCE +3.0% YoY, both below consensus of +3.7% and +3.3% respectively — a genuine beat that sent futures higher and yields slightly lower. ADP September employment also beat at +90,000 vs. 68,000 expected. The AI subplot adds spice: Anthropic's leaked IPO prospectus is targeting a $2 trillion valuation and disclosing $518 billion in decade-long infrastructure commitments. Today is the last trading day of September and Q3, meaning rebalancing flows will dominate the afternoon — watch for late-session volatility.

01 What Moved Markets Today

Yields at 19-Year Highs, Consumer Confidence at 12-Year Lows

Treasury Yields

The 10-year Treasury yield touched 5.29% intraday Tuesday — its highest level since mid-2007 — before settling around 5.25%. The 30-year briefly crossed 5.62%, a level not seen since 2002. Rising yields compressed equity valuations and created a powerful gravitational pull on risk assets, with the S&P 500 closing at 7,670.84 (−0.17%) and the Dow at 51,349.92 (−0.26%) for a second consecutive down day.

Consumer Confidence

The Conference Board's September consumer confidence index fell to 81.9, well below August's 88.6 reading — a 12-year low. The present situation index dropped to 109.3 from a revised 117.2 in August, and the expectations index came in at 63.6 against a forecast of 68.5. Weakness in confidence signals that consumers are beginning to feel the strain of elevated borrowing costs and persistent inflation.

AI & IPO Market

A leaked Anthropic IPO prospectus dominated tech headlines, revealing the AI startup is targeting a valuation near $2 trillion and has committed to $518 billion in AI infrastructure spending over the next decade. Separately, Axios reported that OpenAI's annual recurring revenue is approaching $70 billion — up more than 70% since the start of Q3. Health tech startup Oura shelved its planned IPO citing market uncertainty, underscoring how surging yields are closing the IPO window.

Nvidia & Mega-Cap Tech

Nvidia closed lower by 0.7% on Tuesday despite announcing a $150 billion share repurchase authorization — its largest ever. The buyback was announced Monday and initially lifted shares 1.6%, but the yield environment erased those gains by the following session. Broadcom rose 1.6%, Bloom Energy surged 10.9% on AI power demand, while Tesla fell roughly 4% and Meta dropped nearly 5% on Monday.

02 Why It Mattered

When the Risk-Free Rate Hits 5.25%, Everything Gets Repriced

Here is the core tension gripping markets right now: the U.S. government will pay you 5.25% annually with zero default risk. That number — the 10-year Treasury yield — is the foundation against which every other asset in the world is valued. When it rises, the math on stocks, real estate, private equity, and leveraged corporate balance sheets all deteriorates simultaneously. Tuesday's intraday high of 5.29% wasn't just a statistic — it was a 19-year high watermark that forced traders to ask an uncomfortable question: how much of the equity rally since early 2026 was justified, and how much was priced on the assumption that rates would stay lower?

The answer is complicated by the Iran war. The U.S.-Iran conflict is keeping oil prices elevated — WTI traded above $90/barrel — which feeds directly into headline inflation and makes the Fed's 2% target feel increasingly remote. Treasury Secretary Bessent's efforts to talk down bond yields have failed to gain traction. The 2-year yield sat near 4.88% on Tuesday, a level that prices in at least one additional Fed rate hike in 2026. Market bets on an October hike climbed to roughly 70%.

For equity investors, the math is unforgiving. Higher discount rates shrink the present value of future cash flows — which is why long-duration assets like growth stocks and unprofitable tech are most vulnerable. The S&P 500's year-to-date gain has been sustained largely by mega-cap technology, but as yields approach or breach 5.5% on longer maturities, that narrow support base becomes a liability, not a cushion. Tuesday's session — where Nasdaq only slipped 0.09% while the Dow fell 0.26% — tells you money is still crowding into a narrow slice of the market.

The 30-year yield above 5.5% matters for mortgage markets too. The average 30-year fixed mortgage rate is approaching 7.6%, closing in on a three-year high. That is not a housing market story — it is a consumer balance sheet story.

04 What It Means

A Cooler PCE vs. a Still-Hot World: Where Do We Go From Here?

For Rates

The August PCE beat (headline +3.4% YoY vs. +3.7% expected; core +3.0% YoY vs. +3.3% expected) reduces near-term pressure on the Fed to hike in October. However, the monthly acceleration — headline PCE rose +0.3% MoM, up from +0.1% in July — shows the trend is not unambiguously moving toward 2%. One good month does not make a pivot.

For Equities

Today's softer PCE and a strong ADP print (+90K vs. 68K expected) give bulls a story: disinflation is intact, the labor market is healthy, and yields may have peaked for now. But the S&P 500 has lost 0.5% in September and the Dow is down 4.3% for the month. End-of-quarter rebalancing could amplify moves in either direction this afternoon.

For the Economy

Real consumer spending rose +0.6% MoM in August — the strongest monthly increase in over a year. But the personal saving rate fell to 4.1%, a nearly four-year low. Consumers are spending, but depleting reserves to do it. That is a resilience story with an expiration date.

What to Watch

Friday's nonfarm payrolls report is the defining event of the next week. A print above 200K keeps hike odds elevated. A weak print below 100K opens the door for yields to fall and equities to rally. Today, watch whether the 10-year yield can sustain below 5.20% — if it does, the equity market will take notice.

Key Watch Item

The 10-year Treasury yield. It eased to ~5.22% early Wednesday after PCE data. If it closes below 5.20%, that is the first meaningful technical support level and could mark a short-term peak. A close above 5.30% would be a new signal that the bond selloff has further to run — and equities would react accordingly.

TECHNOLOGY Relative Outperformer

Nasdaq fell only 0.09% on Tuesday while the broader S&P 500 dropped 0.17%. Broadcom (AVGO) gained 1.6% and Bloom Energy (BE) surged 10.9% on AI power demand enthusiasm. Money continues to crowd into a narrow slice of mega-cap tech, even as yields rise — a fragile dynamic that makes the sector both a leader and a concentration risk.

FINANCIALS −6.3% MTD

The S&P 500 financials sector is down 6.3% in September — its worst monthly performance since March 2023 and on pace to snap a four-month winning streak. While higher rates theoretically benefit bank net interest margins, the speed of the yield move has created mark-to-market losses on bond portfolios and raised credit risk concerns. Financials are approaching their 200-day moving average.

ENERGY Watch

Oil above $90/barrel should be a tailwind for energy stocks, but the sector saw broad losses Tuesday — Exxon Mobil fell 0.7%. Higher energy prices feed inflation, which in turn drives yields up and compresses the multiples that energy companies trade at. It is a contradictory dynamic: the commodity benefits, but rising rates punish the equity. Watch whether WTI holds above $90 today.

06 Finance Career Lens

The Rate Shock Has Something to Teach Every Finance Track

Across all tracks

When the 10-year Treasury yield crosses 5%, it is not just a bond market story — it is the single most important variable in corporate finance, portfolio construction, and wealth planning. Every finance professional needs a clear mental model for how rate shocks ripple through their domain.

Wealth Management & Private Banking

Clients with mortgage debt approaching 7.6% rates and equity portfolios under pressure are anxious. The move into short-duration fixed income — T-bills at 4.47%, 2-year notes at 4.89% — is the conversation you will have in every client meeting. The key message: higher-for-longer rates mean fixed income is genuinely competitive with equities for the first time in a generation. Know how to frame the risk/reward comparison clearly, and understand how to position bond ladders when the yield curve is inverted.

Equity Research

A 5.25% risk-free rate changes the DCF math materially. If you are covering a growth stock with terminal cash flows discounted at 8%, the equity risk premium just got squeezed. Know how to rebuild your models with updated WACC assumptions and be ready to explain what a 50-basis-point yield rise does to your price target. Analysts who can articulate the rate sensitivity of their coverage universe — not just the earnings story — will stand out in this environment.

Investment Banking

The IPO window is testing again. Oura shelved its offering Tuesday citing uncertainty. Anthropic's leaked prospectus reveals it is eyeing a post-midterms listing — rational, given that a rate peak would help its $2 trillion valuation narrative land more credibly. In M&A, rising borrowing costs squeeze LBO feasibility and compress deal multiples. Know how to run a quick sensitivity analysis on entry multiples vs. exit rates — this is exactly what bankers are modeling right now for leveraged buyout clients.

07 Why This Matters for Interviews

Five Things an Interviewer Could Ask You About Yields and Equities Right Now

  • "Walk me through what happens to equity valuations when the risk-free rate rises from 4% to 5.25%." — The core mechanism: higher discount rates lower the present value of future cash flows. Quantify it: a 125-basis-point rise in the risk-free rate can compress a stock's fair value by 15–25% depending on duration of cash flows. Long-duration growth stocks suffer most.
  • A strong answer would include: The concept of equity risk premium (ERP), why the ERP matters as much as the absolute yield level, and how this dynamic explains why tech (which already trades at high multiples) can underperform even when its business is fine.
  • "PCE came in below expectations this morning — is the Fed done hiking?" — Don't overclaim. One softer data point doesn't change the trend. Monthly PCE accelerated (+0.3% MoM). The Fed watches the sustained trend, not a single print. A balanced answer says: it reduces October hike odds, but Friday's payrolls data matters more.
  • Don't say: "The market is going to rally now." Predicting market direction in an interview signals overconfidence. Say instead: "The data reduces rate risk at the margin, but the path forward depends on how the labor market develops."
08 Term of the Day
Bear Steepener

A bear steepener is a yield curve move in which long-end yields rise faster than short-end yields, causing the spread between them to widen — in an upward direction — while overall yields increase. It is called "bear" because rising yields mean falling bond prices, hurting bondholders. It is called "steepener" because the curve becomes steeper (longer maturities yield progressively more than shorter ones). Today's environment is a textbook example: the 10-year Treasury rose sharply to 5.25%–5.29%, while the 2-year moved to ~4.89% — meaning the long end is moving more aggressively. A bear steepener typically reflects rising inflation expectations or concerns about long-term fiscal sustainability, both of which are active narratives right now given the Iran war, oil prices, and the U.S. deficit debate.

How to Talk About the Yield Move Without Sounding Alarmist

In a coffee chat, class, or interview

"This morning's PCE print was genuinely encouraging — headline at 3.4% and core at 3.0% both came in below consensus — but it doesn't change the structural picture. The 10-year yield hit 5.29% on Tuesday, its highest since 2007, and that level is already doing the Fed's work for it by tightening financial conditions. The real test is Friday's payrolls report. A hot jobs number would put October rate hike odds — already near 70% — back on the table, and that would be the next pressure point for equities and the IPO market."
