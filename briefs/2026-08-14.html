Now the week's final data point lands: July Retail Sales at 8:30 AM ET. After a surprisingly weak jobs report last week and cooling PPI, the consumer number is the swing factor between 'soft landing confirmed' and 'demand is cracking.'

00 Smart in 30 Seconds
The whole issue in a paragraph

The S&P 500 closed at a record 7,798.99 on Thursday — its 27th all-time high of 2026 — after July PPI came in flat on a monthly basis (well below the +0.2% consensus), building on Wednesday's in-line CPI print of +3.4% YoY. Two consecutive soft inflation reads cut the probability of a September Fed rate hike from roughly 52% a week ago to around 32%, sending yields lower and equities higher. Semiconductors led the charge: Sandisk surged 13.7%, Micron rose 4.2%, and the Nasdaq added 0.81% to 26,803.03. Not everything worked — Cisco fell ~9% after earnings missed elevated expectations, and Cerebras dropped 11.85%. After the close, Applied Materials beat Q3 estimates (EPS $3.50 vs. $3.40; revenue $9.12B vs. $8.99B) and guided Q4 revenue above consensus, but the stock is down roughly 5% in premarket on weaker-than-expected reported operating income tied to unrealized investment losses. The session's critical swing factor: July Retail Sales hit at 8:30 AM ET, with early reads pointing to an unexpected 0.6% decline that could reset soft-landing assumptions fast.

01 What Moved Markets Today

Flat PPI, Falling Oil, and a Semiconductor Surge Lift the S&P 500 to Its First Close Above 7,780

Inflation Data — PPI

July's Producer Price Index came in flat on a monthly basis, below the +0.2% consensus and well below June's revised reading. Year-over-year, headline wholesale inflation slowed to +4.7% from a revised +5.0% in June. Core PPI rose just +0.2% MoM. The data cemented the view that the inflation surge that alarmed markets earlier in the summer is genuinely cooling — and markets moved immediately on the repricing of Fed rate-hike odds.

Semiconductors

Chip stocks extended their winning streak, with Sandisk surging 13.7%, Micron climbing 4.2%, and Marvell rising 3.6%. Reports that Anthropic is pursuing an IPO and seeking to acquire Decart AI for $6 billion added fresh momentum to AI-related spending optimism. The Nasdaq-100 added 1.15% on the day, outperforming the broader Nasdaq Composite's 0.81% gain.

Oil Prices

WTI crude settled at $81.25/barrel, down more than 2% on the session, as the International Energy Agency signaled it now expects global oil demand to fall more than previously forecast. Lower oil reduces input-cost pressure across the economy — a secondary disinflationary signal that reinforced the broader bullish tape. Brent also shed more than 2% to close near $87.07.

Earnings — Losers

Cisco Systems fell approximately 9% after issuing an upbeat revenue forecast that nonetheless fell short of investors' elevated expectations. Because the Dow is price-weighted, Cisco's decline dragged the blue-chip index down by roughly 64 points at its worst — accounting for the bulk of the Dow's muted +0.13% gain versus the S&P 500's 0.65% advance. Cerebras Systems dropped 11.85% after its own earnings disappointed.

02 Why It Mattered

The Fed Is Losing Its Hawkish Cover — and Markets Know It

A single soft inflation print can be noise. Two in a row — CPI at +3.4% YoY on Wednesday, PPI flat MoM on Thursday — starts to look like a trend. The Fed's problem was never that inflation was spiraling higher indefinitely; it was that the pace of deceleration was too slow to justify a pivot. After this week, that calculus has shifted. Money markets now price only a ~33% chance of a September hike, down from nearly 52% just one week ago when traders were still digesting the late-July FOMC signals.

That repricing has a direct transmission mechanism into equities. Lower rate-hike odds reduce the discount rate applied to future earnings — particularly for long-duration growth stocks like semiconductors and software. That is exactly why the Nasdaq outperformed the Dow Thursday: semis benefit most from rate relief because their earnings are weighted furthest into the future. The XLK technology ETF is now up 32.5% year-to-date, and the S&P 500 itself has gained approximately 14% in 2026.

The breadth of Thursday's rally was also notable. Advancing S&P 500 stocks outnumbered decliners by roughly 1.8-to-1, and the Russell 2000 small-cap index hit its own record above 3,060 — up more than 23% year-to-date. When small caps join the party, it's a signal that the rally is not just the top seven tech names doing all the work. That kind of broad participation is usually a healthier sign for sustained upside.

The July 28–29 FOMC meeting produced a 9-to-3 vote to hold rates steady. Minutes from that meeting release on August 19 — the next major event for rate-path watchers.

04 What It Means

Records, Rate Relief, and a Consumer Test — What Each Asset Class Is Pricing

For Rates

The 10-year yield at ~4.66% has pulled back from its recent peak near 4.75%, but it remains elevated by any historical standard. The 30-year Treasury was auctioned this week at the highest rate in a quarter century — investors are demanding a hefty premium to fund the U.S. deficit. If Retail Sales disappoint, expect another leg lower in yields, which would steepen the bull-flattener dynamic already underway.

For Equities

The S&P 500 is up ~14% in 2026 and just set its 27th record close of the year. Valuations are stretched — the index is trading above 7,800 with a market that was priced for perfection just months ago. A soft Retail Sales number is a double-edged sword: it reinforces the rate-hold thesis (bullish for duration), but it also raises questions about earnings growth if the consumer softens.

For the Economy

The week's data tells a coherent story: inflation is cooling (CPI +3.4% YoY, PPI +4.7% YoY and falling), but it remains above the Fed's 2% target. Employment cracked last week — July nonfarm payrolls fell by 23,000 against a forecast gain of ~83,000. If Retail Sales also disappoint today, the soft-landing narrative gets harder to defend. The Fed's hawks lose ammunition, but so does the 'strong growth' argument.

What to Watch

Beyond today's Retail Sales print, the next major catalysts are: FOMC minutes on August 19 (will the 3 dissenting hawks' views be as aggressive as feared?), Walmart earnings on August 20 (the ultimate consumer barometer), and Nvidia's report the week after — the definitive AI-spending check-in that could reset the entire semiconductor thesis.

Key Watch Item

July Retail Sales at 8:30 AM ET. Consensus: +0.3% MoM. Early estimates flag a possible surprise decline of 0.6% MoM. A miss this large — after a jobs miss and two soft inflation prints — would mark a meaningful inflection in the growth picture and force a reassessment of Q3 earnings estimates across consumer-facing sectors.

06 Finance Career Lens

What This Week's Tape Teaches Recruits About Markets, Models, and Macro

Across all tracks

This week is a masterclass in sequential data interpretation: no single print tells the story, but CPI → PPI → Retail Sales in five trading days creates a macro narrative that every desk will be discussing. Know the sequence and what each data point tests.

Wealth Management & Private Banking

Client conversations this week center on one question: is the rate cycle turning? Your answer should be nuanced. Two soft inflation prints reduce near-term rate-hike risk — good for bond prices and growth equity allocations. But with yields still elevated and the consumer showing possible cracks, the argument for holding some duration against higher-rate scenarios remains valid. Avoid over-rotating on two data points.

Equity Research

The Cisco earnings story is your case study for understanding the difference between an absolute beat and a relative disappointment. Cisco issued an upbeat revenue forecast — but it missed the elevated expectations baked into its stock after the AI-infrastructure narrative pushed networking valuations to multi-year highs. When modeling earnings, always ask: what is the market already pricing in? The consensus estimate is a floor, not a target.

Investment Banking

The Anthropic IPO pursuit — reportedly seeking a $6 billion acquisition of Decart AI alongside its own IPO process — is an active deal-flow signal. AI-adjacent M&A is running hot. If you are recruiting into tech M&A or growth equity advisory, you should be able to speak to the consolidation dynamics in AI: why acqui-hires of AI startups are accelerating, what strategic buyers are paying for, and how the IPO window intersects with private-market valuations.

07 Why This Matters for Interviews

Four Things an Interviewer Could Ask You About This Week's Markets

  • Q: The S&P 500 just hit a record despite inflation still being above 2% and the Fed possibly hiking. How do you reconcile that? A strong answer acknowledges that markets are forward-looking — equities rallied not because inflation is solved, but because the pace of deceleration reduced the probability of the next rate hike. Risk assets price the direction of policy, not its level.
  • Q: Why did Cisco fall on what looked like decent earnings? The stock was priced for outperformance — not merely adequate performance. In a high-multiple, high-expectation environment, meeting guidance isn't the same as exceeding market pricing. The lesson: always separate reported results from what was already baked into the share price.
  • Q: What does the PPI tell you that the CPI doesn't? PPI measures what producers pay for inputs — it is a leading indicator of future consumer price pressure. A flat PPI suggests pipeline inflation is cooling, which gives the Fed more confidence that CPI will continue declining without additional hikes. CPI measures what consumers ultimately pay.
  • What NOT to say: Don't call the S&P 500 record a sign that everything is great. The market is pricing a best-case scenario — cooling inflation, no recession, soft landing, rate cuts eventually. A weak Retail Sales print or a hawkish FOMC minutes release could reprice all of those assumptions quickly.
08 Term of the Day
Bull Flattener

A bull flattener occurs when long-end Treasury yields fall faster than short-end yields, causing the yield curve to flatten. It typically signals that markets expect slowing growth or falling inflation — and it is the dynamic playing out right now. Thursday's 10-year yield dropped to ~4.66% as soft PPI data reduced long-run inflation expectations, while the 2-year yield (more anchored to near-term Fed policy) moved less. In a bull flattener, bond prices rise (hence 'bull'), and rate-sensitive equities like REITs and dividend stocks tend to benefit alongside long-duration growth stocks.
