Oil Craters, Dow Hits Record, and Palantir Blows the Roof Off

Monday's session was one of the cleanest risk-on days of the year: WTI crude fell more than 5% on hopes that a U.S.-Iran agreement could reopen the Strait of Hormuz, pulling the inflation premium out of bond yields and sending the Dow to its first all-time closing high in months. Palantir poured fuel on the fire after the bell, reporting Q2 revenue up 93% year-over-year and lifting its full-year guidance — sending the stock surging in premarket. Now all eyes turn to SpaceX's first-ever earnings report as a public company and AMD's results, both due after Tuesday's close.

00 Smart in 30 Seconds
The whole issue in a paragraph

Monday's session was driven by a single macro lever: the inflation risk premium. President Trump announced he was holding off on strikes against Iran after deal terms were agreed in principle, which sent WTI crude down 5.1% to $80.34/barrel and pulled the 10-year Treasury yield down roughly 5 basis points to ~4.68% — backing away from its 18-month high of 4.73–4.74% hit last week. Lower yields lifted rate-sensitive growth stocks, and the Nasdaq surged 2.13% while the Dow logged a new all-time closing high of 53,178. After the bell, Palantir delivered Q2 revenue of $1.94 billion — up 93% year-over-year and ~8% above the $1.80B consensus — and raised its full-year U.S. commercial revenue outlook to above $3.42B, sending the stock sharply higher in premarket trading. Today's main event: SpaceX reports its first-ever quarterly results as a public company after the close, alongside AMD — two reports that will either cement or stress-test the AI infrastructure thesis for the rest of the year.

01 What Moved Markets Today

Three Forces Aligned: Hormuz Diplomacy, Falling Oil, and Palantir's Blowout

Geopolitics / Energy

President Trump posted on Truth Social that Iran had agreed to deal parameters, prompting him to cancel a planned military strike. Treasury Secretary Bessent reinforced the message, saying a U.S.-Iran agreement to reopen the Strait of Hormuz was within reach. WTI crude futures settled down 5.1% at $80.34/barrel — their sharpest single-day drop in weeks — while Brent fell 4.7% to $83.77. The move drained the geopolitical risk premium that had been baked into oil since hostilities escalated earlier this year.

Macro / Rates

With oil pulling back sharply, near-term inflation fears cooled. The 10-year Treasury yield retreated to approximately 4.68%, down roughly 5 basis points from a fresh 18-month high of 4.73–4.74% set last week. Lower yields reduced the discount rate on future earnings, providing direct support to long-duration growth stocks. The ISM Manufacturing Index also came in at 55.6 for July — its strongest reading in more than four years — adding a positive economic growth signal to the mix.

Earnings / After-Hours

Palantir reported Q2 revenue of $1.94 billion, up 93% year-over-year and ahead of the $1.80B consensus estimate. Adjusted EPS of 41 cents beat the 35-cent estimate. U.S. commercial revenue surged 149% year-over-year (YoY) to $764 million, and the company raised its full-year U.S. commercial revenue guidance to above $3.42 billion, up from the prior $3.22 billion outlook. The stock surged sharply in premarket Tuesday. Note: Palantir's GAAP net income of $1.07B (vs. ~$329M a year ago) includes a $0.03/share boost from unrealized gains on its SpaceX stake following SpaceX's June IPO — the core operational beat is real, but the headline GAAP figure is not purely operational.

Overnight / Premarket

Asian markets followed Wall Street's lead: the Nikkei rose 202 points to 63,957 and the Shanghai Composite added 12 points to 3,822, while the Hang Seng slipped 156 points. U.S. futures are pointing higher ahead of Tuesday's open, with Nasdaq futures up more than 0.7% toward 29,000. S&P 500 futures are modestly positive, within striking distance of a fresh all-time high. Treasury yields are edging slightly higher this morning alongside a modest crude oil recovery — worth watching for any reversal of Monday's relief rally.

02 Why It Mattered

The Hormuz Discount: What a Chokepoint Is Worth to Markets

The Strait of Hormuz is the single most important oil chokepoint on earth — roughly 20% of global petroleum liquids flow through its narrow passage. When it was effectively closed or threatened earlier this year, markets priced in a sustained energy shock: WTI surged, inflation breakevens widened, and the Fed faced pressure to hike rather than cut. The 10-year yield climbed 30 basis points during July alone, reaching levels not seen in 18 months. That's not just a bond market story — it's a valuation story for every growth stock in the S&P 500.

Monday's move unwound a meaningful portion of that premium in a single session. The logic is straightforward: if Hormuz reopens, oil supply normalizes, inflation pressure eases, and the Fed's calculus shifts. Markets weren't waiting for a signed agreement — they were pricing the probability of one. With Bessent publicly signaling a deal was close and Polymarket contracts implying a 77% chance the S&P 500 opens higher Tuesday, sentiment flipped hard.

The Palantir report added a second, independent tailwind. After a volatile July during which AI stocks sold off on concerns that heavy capital expenditure wasn't translating into commercial revenue, Palantir's numbers were a direct rebuttal. U.S. commercial revenue up 149% YoY and remaining deal value more than doubling to $6.24 billion shows enterprise AI adoption is accelerating — not stalling. That's exactly what the market needed heading into AMD and SpaceX's results tonight.

Context: The S&P 500 ended Monday just ~20 points below its all-time intraday high. The Dow's all-time closing high was the fourth consecutive day of gains for that index. Seven of the S&P 500's 11 sectors still closed in negative territory — the rally was narrow, concentrated in technology and energy-adjacent names.

04 What It Means

Four Implications for the Week Ahead

For Rates

The 10-year yield's pullback to ~4.68% is relief, not reversal. It climbed 30 bps during July and is still near multi-year highs. Markets are pricing approximately a 65% chance of a 25 bps Fed hike in September — Chair Kevin Warsh has been deliberately vague on forward guidance, and three FOMC members dissented at the July hold. If the Hormuz deal holds and oil stays subdued, the September hike probability could fade. If talks collapse, yields could re-test 4.73%+.

For Equities

The S&P 500 sits within 20 points of its all-time intraday high — a record close is in reach if Tuesday's earnings (SpaceX, AMD) deliver. But note the composition: only four of 11 S&P 500 sectors closed in positive territory Monday. The rally remains narrow. A broadening into cyclicals and small caps (Russell 2000 was up 1.73% Monday) would signal genuine risk-on durability rather than another AI-concentrated pop.

For the Economy

The ISM Manufacturing Index hitting 55.6 in July — its strongest reading in more than four years — is a meaningful signal. Combined with lower oil, easing inflation pressure, and solid corporate earnings (approximately 86% of S&P 500 reporters beating estimates this season), the macro backdrop looks constructive. The risk is that the Hormuz deal is fragile and oil could spike again rapidly if talks break down.

What to Watch

SpaceX reports its first-ever quarterly earnings after Tuesday's bell. As the largest IPO in history (debuting at ~$1.75T and briefly surging 65% before retreating), its results carry significant weight for the AI infrastructure narrative. AMD's chip results will directly speak to GPU demand and data center capex. Both reports follow the template Palantir set Monday: guidance matters more than the headline beat.

Key Watch Item

Friday's nonfarm payrolls report is the week's most important macro data point. A strong jobs number would complicate the case against a September Fed rate hike, potentially pushing yields back up toward their 18-month highs. A soft print could do the opposite — and extend Monday's bond rally.

05 Market Sector Lens

Technology Led, Energy Retreated, and Breadth Stayed Thin

Technology (XLK) Leading

AI software and semiconductor names drove the session. Palantir's after-hours surge extended Monday's tech leadership. The Nasdaq's 2.13% gain was its best single-day move in weeks, snapping a volatile July. Advancers beat decliners better than two-to-one across the broader market, with AI and technology names concentrated at the top of the leaderboard.

Energy (XLE) Lagging

WTI crude's 5.1% drop was unambiguously bad for energy sector earnings. E&P companies, refiners, and oilfield services names faced direct margin pressure as the commodity moved sharply lower. The sector underperformed despite the broad market rally — a textbook negative correlation between oil prices and the rest of the market reasserting itself.

Industrials / Defense Watch

Defense names face a mixed signal: de-escalation in the Middle East reduces near-term conflict premium, but a $76.6B Navy contract award for Columbia- and Virginia-class submarines announced during the session provided a countervailing positive. Caterpillar, which reports Tuesday, will be a key read on global industrial demand and infrastructure spending.

06 Finance Career Lens

What Geopolitical Risk Premiums Mean for Every Finance Track

Across all tracks

Monday's session is a live case study in how geopolitical events create and destroy risk premiums across asset classes simultaneously — oil, bonds, equities, and FX all moved in a coordinated direction the moment one news item changed the probability distribution for energy supply. Understanding that transmission mechanism is foundational finance.

Wealth Management & Private Banking

Clients with significant energy sector exposure just saw a sharp drawdown in a single session. The question your senior advisor is asking: was Monday's oil move a structural de-risking or a temporary relief rally? That conversation requires you to understand the difference between a risk premium compression event and a fundamental supply shift. Be ready to explain why the Dow can hit a record on the same day energy stocks fall.

Equity Research

Palantir's result is a direct test of your AI sector thesis. If you've been modeling U.S. commercial AI software revenue growth at 50–80% YoY and Palantir just printed 149% YoY, your model needs updating. More importantly, remaining deal value more than doubling to $6.24B is a leading indicator — that's future revenue already under contract. Analysts covering the software sector should be revising estimates and price targets before the open today.

Investment Banking

SpaceX's debut earnings report is a landmark event for the IPO market. The company went public in June as the largest IPO in history at ~$1.75 trillion, surged 65%, then pulled back below its IPO price in July. Tonight's numbers will either validate the listing valuation or deepen the post-IPO hangover — a dynamic every ECM banker needs to understand going into conversations with prospective issuers about timing, pricing, and lock-up risk.

07 Why This Matters for Interviews

Questions You Could Face This Week — and How to Answer Them Well

  • "Walk me through what happened in markets on Monday and why." — Lead with the macro catalyst (Hormuz deal, oil −5%), explain the transmission (lower oil → lower inflation expectations → yields drop → growth stocks rally), then add the micro layer (Palantir earnings beat validates AI demand). Three layers, one clean narrative.
  • A strong answer includes: The specific numbers — WTI at $80.34/barrel (−5.1%), 10Y yield at ~4.68% (−5 bps), Nasdaq +2.13%, Dow all-time high at 53,178. Specificity signals you actually read the tape, not just the headlines.
  • Follow-up angle: "Why did only four of eleven S&P 500 sectors close positive despite the Dow hitting a record?" The answer: the rally was concentrated in technology, not broad. Records can be narrow. An interviewer who asks this is testing whether you understand index composition and market breadth.
  • Don't say: "Markets rallied because of good news." That's not analysis. Trace the mechanism: risk premium compression in the energy market flowed through to lower yields, which changed the discount rate, which repriced long-duration assets. That's what a finance professional sounds like.
08 Term of the Day
Geopolitical Risk Premium

A geopolitical risk premium is the extra return investors demand — or the extra price embedded in an asset — to compensate for the possibility of an adverse geopolitical event. In commodity markets, it typically shows up as oil trading above its fundamental supply-demand clearing price when conflict threatens a major supply route. On Monday, the Strait of Hormuz risk premium compressed sharply: when Trump signaled a deal was in reach, the probability of a sustained oil supply disruption fell, and WTI dropped 5.1% in a single session — not because supply or demand fundamentals changed, but because the probability-weighted risk of disruption changed. The same dynamic runs in reverse through Treasury yields and equity discount rates, which is why a geopolitical de-escalation can simultaneously drive bonds higher, yields lower, and stocks to all-time highs.
