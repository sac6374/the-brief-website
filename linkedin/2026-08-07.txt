Friday's July jobs report hit like a thunderclap: the U.S. economy shed 23,000 jobs — more than 106,000 below consensus — and prior months were revised down by a combined 103,000. Treasury yields dropped, stocks rallied, and the probability of a September Fed hike collapsed. For finance students and early-career professionals, this is exactly the kind of macro surprise that reshapes interview conversations overnight. Understanding why a bad jobs number can be good for equities — and bad in ways that aren't obvious yet — is the edge that separates prepared candidates from the crowd.
